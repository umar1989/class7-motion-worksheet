1 · How to Write a Full-Marks Answer

In a written exam, the teacher gives marks for steps, not only the final answer. A correct answer with no working may lose half the marks.

A. For numerical problems (3–4 marks)

1 · GivenWrite all values with units. Draw a small diagram if the path has turns.
2 · FormulaWrite the formula in symbols first.
3 · SubstitutePut the numbers in, with units.
4 · AnswerFinal value + unit + direction (for displacement / velocity).

B. For theory questions

Question wordWhat to writeMarks tip
Define1 exact sentence using the keyword (e.g. “scalar”, “rate of change of displacement”).Keyword = mark
DifferentiateA table with at least 3 points, side by side.1 mark per point
Give reason / Why?Statement + “because” + example.Reason + example
ExplainDefinition → example → conclusion.Use real-life example
Mark-losing mistakes: forgetting units · forgetting direction in displacement/velocity · writing “speed” when the question asks “velocity” · writing the formula in words only · not showing the diagram when the path turns.

2 · Learn These (Key Definitions)

TermExam-ready definition
RestAn object is at rest if its position does not change with respect to its surroundings.
MotionAn object is in motion if its position changes with time with respect to its surroundings.
Rest & motion are relativeAn object can be at rest for one observer and in motion for another. Eg: a passenger is at rest with respect to the bus but in motion with respect to a person on the road.
DistanceThe total path length travelled by an object. Scalar. Never negative. SI unit: metre (m).
DisplacementThe shortest straight-line distance from the initial to the final position, in a given direction. Vector. Displacement = final position − initial position. SI unit: metre (m).
SpeedDistance travelled per unit time. Scalar. Speed = distance ÷ time. SI unit: m/s.
VelocityDisplacement per unit time (rate of change of displacement). Vector. Velocity = displacement ÷ time. SI unit: m/s.
Uniform motionEqual distances covered in equal intervals of time. Eg: minute hand of a clock.
Non-uniform motionUnequal distances covered in equal intervals of time. Eg: a bus in traffic.
Uniform velocityEqual displacements in equal times in the same direction.
Instantaneous speedSpeed of a body at a particular instant. Eg: speedometer reading.

Types of motion

TypeMeaningExamples
TranslatoryAll parts move the same distance in the same direction (no rotation).Car on straight road, book sliding on table
CurvilinearTranslatory motion along a curved path.Car taking a turn, thrown ball
RotatoryEvery part moves in a circle around a fixed axis.Ceiling fan, spinning top, Earth on its axis
Oscillatory“To and fro” motion about a fixed point, along the same path.Pendulum, swing, sitar string

Formulas & conversions

Speed = Distance ÷ Time  |  Velocity = Displacement ÷ Time
1 km/h = 5/18 m/s   |   1 m/s = 18/5 km/h
Shortcut: km/h → m/s : multiply by 5/18. m/s → km/h : multiply by 18/5.
Remember: Displacement ≤ Distance, always. So distance ÷ displacement is always ≥ 1. They are equal only for straight-line motion in one direction.

4 · Model Answers (Study How Marks Are Earned)

Model 1 — Distance & displacement with a turn (4 marks)

Q. A person walks 4 m east, then 3 m north. Find the distance and displacement.

Given: 4 m east, then 3 m north (right-angled path).
Distance = 4 m + 3 m = 7 m (1 mark)
Displacement = straight line from start to end = √(4² + 3²) (1 mark: method)
= √(16 + 9) = √25 = 5 m (1 mark) towards north-east (north of east) (1 mark: unit + direction)

Model 2 — Closed loop (3 marks)

Q. A student runs once around a circular park and returns to the start. Path covered = 200 m. Find distance and displacement.

Distance = 200 m (whole path counted). (1)
Displacement = final position − initial position = 0 m. (1)
Reason: starting point and ending point are the same. (1)

Model 3 — Speed and velocity (4 marks)

Q. A car travels from A to B in 2 hours. The distance AB is 120 km. Find the speed and velocity.

Given: d = 120 km, t = 2 h
Formula: speed v = d / t (1)
v = 120 km ÷ 2 h = 60 km/h (1)
Velocity = displacement / time. For straight-line motion, displacement = 120 km, so velocity = 120 ÷ 2 = 60 km/h from A towards B (2: formula, direction)

Model 4 — Theory “Differentiate” answer (3 marks)

Q. Differentiate between speed and velocity.

Draw the table from Section 3 and write any three points: scalar vs vector, distance vs displacement, no direction vs has direction. Add the example “40 km/h” and “40 km/h towards North” to earn the last mark.

6 · Answer Key & Marking Points

Try everything first. Then compare your working step by step. Marks are given for each step.

Part A answers
  1. scalar; vector
  2. metre per second (m/s)
  3. True
  4. 72 × 5/18 = 20 m/s
  5. 15 × 18/5 = 54 km/h
  6. Rotatory motion
  7. Instantaneous speed
  8. No. Displacement is never greater than distance, so distance is never less than displacement.
Part B answers
B1. Motion is the change in position of an object with respect to its surroundings in a given time. Examples: a car moving on a road; a bird flying in the sky.
B2. Distance: total path length; scalar; no direction; always positive; depends on path. Displacement: shortest straight-line path from start to end; vector; has direction; can be +, − or zero; depends only on start and end positions.
B3. An object may be in motion for one observer and at rest for another. Example: a person in a moving bus sees the friend beside them at rest, but a person standing on the road sees both in motion. So rest and motion depend on the observer.
B4. “40 km/h” gives only the magnitude, so it is speed (scalar). “40 km/h towards North” gives magnitude and direction, so it is velocity (vector).
B5. Uniform motion: equal distances in equal intervals of time (minute hand of a clock). Non-uniform motion: unequal distances in equal intervals of time (a bus moving in traffic).
B6. Translatory (car on a straight road); rotatory (ceiling fan); oscillatory (pendulum / swing). Curvilinear (car taking a turn) is a special type of translatory motion.
Part C answers
C1. Distance = 6 + 8 = 14 m. Displacement = √(6² + 8²) = √(36 + 64) = √100 = 10 m, towards north of east.
C2. Distance = 12 + 5 = 17 m. Displacement = 12 − 5 = 7 m forward.
C3. Speed = d/t = 150 ÷ 3 = 50 km/h. Straight line, so displacement = 150 km east. Velocity = 150 ÷ 3 = 50 km/h towards east.
C4. (i) Speed = 400 ÷ 50 = 8 m/s. (ii) Displacement = 0 m (start = end). (iii) Average velocity = 0 ÷ 50 = 0 m/s.
C5. v = 72 km/h = 72 × 5/18 = 20 m/s. Distance = v × t = 20 × 30 = 600 m.
C6. Distance 1 = 20 × 15 = 300 m. Distance 2 = 10 × 10 = 100 m. Total distance = 400 m. Total time = 25 s. Average speed = 400 ÷ 25 = 16 m/s.
C7. Distance = 15 + 20 = 35 km. Displacement = √(15² + 20²) = √(225 + 400) = √625 = 25 km (north of east).
C8. Distance = 20 m; displacement = 0 m. Distance describes the actual movement better, because you really walked 20 m, while displacement only compares the start and end points, which are the same.
Part D answers
D1. (a) Car A: 150 m, 150 m, 150 m, 150 m. Car B: 50 m, 40 m, 90 m, 50 m. (b) Car A is in uniform motion because it covers equal distances (150 m) in equal intervals of time (10 s); Car B covers unequal distances, so it is non-uniform. (c) Speed of A = 150 ÷ 10 = 15 m/s.
D2. U, NU, NU, U, NU, U (in order of the table, row by row).
D3. O, R, T, C, R, O (in order of the table, row by row).
Part E answers
E1. Distance = 150 m. Displacement = 0 m. They are not equal, because the starting and ending points are the same, so there is no change in position, although the whole path is counted in distance.
E2. No. Even though the speed is the same, the direction changes (East to North), and velocity includes direction. So velocity is non-uniform.
E3. With respect to the compartment/other passengers he is at rest, because his position does not change. With respect to a person standing on the platform or the trees outside, he is in motion. So rest depends on the observer.
E4. Yes, displacement can be zero with non-zero distance (a closed loop like a round trip). No, distance cannot be zero if displacement is non-zero, because to change position, some path must be travelled.
